GX-01 Path C — LCD/HAT sandwich display module

Concept: J2 moves from the top (F.Cu) to the bottom (B.Cu) side of the adapter HAT. The LCD hard-solders directly onto those flipped J2 pins. LCD + J2 + HAT become a single rigid assembly — the display module — which mounts to the inside of the front panel as one piece. All other connectors (J1 GPIO ribbon port, J3/J4 fans, RV1 contrast pot, R1, C1) live on the case-interior side of the HAT, accessible without disturbing the LCD.

1. Exploded side view of the display module

Layer-by-layer: ① printed front panel (FDE PETG, 3 mm); ② smoked acrylic window; ③ LCD module with display facing user; ④ flipped J2 pin header — its pins pass through both the LCD's back-side through-holes and the HAT's B.Cu side pads, with solder joints on both PCBs (this is the mechanical/electrical backbone of the assembly); ⑤ adapter HAT PCB (F.Cu side faces the case interior); ⑥ case-interior-facing components — J1 receives the 40-pin ribbon from the HAT stack, J3/J4 host fan leads, RV1 is adjusted through a service hole/port. A printed bracket holds the assembly to the front panel interior at the top and bottom edges.

2. The whole case — display module + HAT stack + cables

The HAT stack ends at LC29H — no adapter HAT above it, so the 10 mm clearance under the exhaust fan is preserved. The display module (LCD + flipped J2 + HAT + back-side components) is a unitary slab against the inside of the front panel. A 40-pin IDC ribbon runs horizontally from LC29H's passthrough to J1 on the HAT's case-interior face. Fan leads exit J3/J4 and route to the top exhaust fan and the front intake fan.

3. Case-interior view of the display module (looking at the HAT's back)

The case-interior side of the HAT. J1 is front and center — where the 40-pin IDC ribbon plugs in from the HAT stack. J3 and J4 host the fan cables. R1, C1, RV1 are all accessible. The 2×20 pad array of J2 is behind the PCB and not visible from this side (the LCD is directly behind, on the other side of the board). The dashed rectangle shows the LCD's footprint — note it extends slightly past the HAT top & bottom edges since the LCD is 75×52.7 mm vs the HAT's 65×56.5 mm.

It still doesn't fit as a simple side-by-side layout. If both the display module AND the HAT stack occupy the same vertical region of the case (bottom-to-top), they collide. The display module's 33 mm of front-depth + the HAT stack's 85 mm of depth + walls = 127 mm, but the case is 100 mm deep.

How this actually works: the vertical void you identified

The reason this concept still works is that the display module and the HAT stack don't need to share vertical space. Looking at the side cross-section (diagram 2 above):

  • The HAT stack sits at the bottom of the case, occupying roughly H=0-65 mm vertically (from X1100 at the bottom up through LC29H at the top).
  • The LCD window is in the upper portion of the front panel. The display module can mount with its vertical center at ~H=55-65 mm, spanning H=28-80 mm (LCD is 52.7 mm tall).
  • In the overlap region (roughly H=28-65 where both the display module and the HAT stack want to occupy space), the HAT stack is narrower in D than the case. Specifically: the AI HAT+ and LC29H are only 56 mm deep, while X1100 (below them) is 85 mm. So at heights above X1207 (~H=30 upward), the stack only needs 56 mm of depth.
  • That leaves 94 − 56 = 38 mm of free depth at the front of the case for H ≥ ~30 mm. The display module's ~33 mm fits with ~5 mm to spare.
  • Below H≈30 (where X1100 and Pi are): only 94 − 85 = 9 mm free at the front. Display module does not extend into this band. Front intake fan fits here.
Verdict: yes, it fits. The display module occupies the upper-front region of the case (roughly H=28-80 mm, D=0-33 mm from front), exactly where the HAT stack is narrower. The lower-front region (H=0-30 mm) hosts the intake fan. The rear region (D=33-89 mm) hosts the X1100 + full HAT stack.

Path C (sandwich) — pros
  • Fits in the current case footprint (no dimension bump)
  • Eliminates the LCD ribbon cable — removes a whole class of "did you plug it in right?" failure modes
  • The LCD+HAT assembly is structurally rigid; vibration and handling don't loosen anything
  • Only one external connection to the display module (the 40-pin IDC ribbon); serviceable as a unit
  • Aesthetic: slab + clean case, nothing chunky
Path C (sandwich) — cons
  • PCB refab: current jlc_bundle.zip is wasted ($80 cost, 10-14 days delay)
  • LCD replacement becomes surgery, not a swap — desolder 20 through-hole joints on both boards
  • Assembly sequence is more specialized: must solder LCD to HAT before mounting to panel; testing the LCD pre-assembly is harder
  • Mounting bracket design: need a printed piece that holds the LCD flush with the window and the HAT at the right standoff distance; one more part to design and print
  • RV1 adjustment needs a side-wall service hole (rubber grommet plug) — small aesthetic scar on the case

6. Open design questions to settle before committing

  1. LCD mounting-hole alignment. SparkFun LCD-00710's datasheet shows the board dimensions; I haven't confirmed the mounting-hole positions vs our adapter HAT's MH positions (H1–H4 at 3.5/61.5 × 3.5/52.5 mm). If they don't align, the printed bracket grips each board independently. That's fine but adds a layer.
  2. J2 pin length. Short straight pins (~2.5 mm both sides) minimize depth but require the LCD to sit nearly flush against the HAT. Longer pins (~8 mm both sides) give a 3-4 mm service gap for solder cleaning and visual inspection but eat depth. Probably 5 mm total height is the sweet spot.
  3. J1 position on the HAT case-interior side. Currently J1 is on F.Cu at the top of the board (rotated 90°, pins along the top edge). For Path C's sandwich, J1 stays on F.Cu (which is now the case-interior side since the board is flipped relative to the stack). The ribbon entering J1 wants to exit toward the back of the case — so J1 may want to move to the back edge of the HAT (opposite edge from the fan connectors). This is a layout revision inside the same PCB footprint.
  4. Test-point access after assembly. Once the LCD is soldered on, probing the J2 signals for debugging requires pogo pins or probe holes in the HAT's case-interior side. Worth adding a few test pads next to R1, RV1, and the LCD control signals while we're already respinning.
  5. Service-hole for RV1. A 4-5 mm diameter hole in the case side wall, with a rubber grommet plug, aligned with RV1's adjustment screw. Trivial to print.
  6. Thermal path. The HAT had previously been a "top of stack" component with good airflow from the exhaust fan. In Path C, it sits tight against the front panel with the LCD sandwich. RV1 and J1 dissipate essentially nothing, so no thermal problem — but worth calling out for completeness.
